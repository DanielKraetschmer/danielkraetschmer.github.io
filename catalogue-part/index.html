Where the stress goes

Every part at once, and the one that matters.

Share of the S235 yield strength, away from the idealised connections. No safety factor was given, so this is a number, not a verdict: the verdict needs your material data and your safety factor.

The axle bends

84 MPa at mid-span.

Beam theory, supported on the fork legs' inner faces, gives 91 MPa. The thin legs hardly clamp the axle: it behaves almost like a simply supported beam.

The fork, with a caveat

59 MPa at sharp inner corners.

Where the legs meet the crown, the CAD model has sharp corners. The error estimate there is 31 %: read it as ±30 %. A real bend radius lowers it.

Document → data → model

Every input has an origin.

The geometry and the load come from the manufacturer's documents. What the documents do not say is marked: typical values and assumptions.

  3. sourceDatasheet

How it works

Four steps, each one a script you can rerun.

  1. 1 · Inputs

    CAD model and datasheet

    Five solids as delivered, the rated load and the wheel materials. Everything else is marked as typical or assumed.

  2. 2 · Model

    Five parts, two contacts

    111 541 nodes, quadratic elements, the tread hyperelastic. Tread on the floor and wheel on the axle as contacts with friction.

  3. 3 · Load

    The floor pushes

    A rigid floor moves up in 14 steps until it carries 1000 N. The steel bends a little: shown here 500 times larger.

  4. 4 · Check

    Against hand calculations

    Balance, moment, contact pressure and axle bending, each compared with an independent formula.

The evidence

Every number comes with its check.

The whole story

Rated 1000 N, in 82 seconds.

Assumptions: Swivel castor, wheel Ø80 mm, rated load 1000 N, shown without its manufacturer; the geometry is the catalogue CAD model as delivered. Steel S235 for fork, plate and axle (E 210 GPa, yield 235 MPa), the tread as a Neo-Hooke material with G = 5 MPa (about 85 Shore A), the wheel core PP: assumed or typical values, not the manufacturer's. The swivel head is tied to the plate (it stands in for the kingpin and the ball races), axle head and nut are bonded to the fork legs (no preload), the floor is rigid and moves up under displacement control. Values at 1000 N are interpolated between the computed steps around it. The first-yield load scales the axle stress linearly from 1000 N: an estimate, since the tread is nonlinear. Peaks at the idealised connections and the sharp fork corners depend on the mesh; no mesh convergence study was made.
