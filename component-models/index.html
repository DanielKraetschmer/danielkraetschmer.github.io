How it works

Four steps, each one a script you can rerun.

  1. 1 · Component

    The package in 3D

    Die, die attach, copper die pad, 32 gull-wing leads and mould: 89 695 unknowns, calibrated to θJC = 20 K/W.

  2. 2 · Ports

    34 thermal ports

    Each lead foot on its pad, the air gap under the body and the body surface: where package and board exchange heat.

  3. 3 · Reduce and couple

    176 + 214 → 357

    Each part reduced on its own, its port temperatures kept. Added at the ports: no iteration, no co-simulation.

  4. 4 · Check

    Against the full models

    Package and board in full 3D, coupled exactly: steady state, heat-up and pulses, node by node.

The whole story

Components as building blocks, in 75 seconds.

Assumptions: TQFP-32 7x7x1.0 mm, 0.8 mm pitch (assumed ATmega328P-AU) with typical materials; the mould conductivity is calibrated so that θJC = 20 K/W. Leads ideally soldered to their pads, a 0.1 mm air gap under the body, convection h = 12 W/m²K on the body surface; the board as on the PCB thermal page. The model is linear: temperature rises scale with power and are added to the ambient temperature, so the calculator's absolute temperatures assume constant material values and convection. 1 W is a reference load, well above what this microcontroller dissipates in normal use. The calculator uses 1 s time steps like the reference runs. Times measured on the same machine; the full models coupled exactly through their port heat flows.
