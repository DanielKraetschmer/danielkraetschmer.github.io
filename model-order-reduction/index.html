How it works

Four steps, each one a script you can rerun.

  1. 1 · Full model

    334 889 unknowns

    The checked thermal model of the board, built from its Gerber files: layered elements, connected everywhere.

  2. 2 · Matrices

    K and C from CalculiX

    Conductivity, heat capacity and the load, exported by the solver. They reproduce its own result to 5·10⁻⁵ K.

  3. 3 · Reduction

    21 vectors, 7 time scales

    A Krylov projection captures the response from 1 ms to 1000 s. No fitting, no training data.

  4. 4 · Check

    Against the full system

    The heat-up and a new pulsed load, compared node by node with the full model.

The whole story

From hours of FEM to milliseconds, in 71 seconds.

Assumptions: the thermal model of the PCB thermal page, linear (constant material values and convection), so the reduced model is valid for this layout, this cooling and these typical material values; change one and the reduction is rebuilt in minutes. Temperatures are rises above ambient air at the hottest of the 32 U1 pads. Times measured on the same machine: CalculiX on 8 cores for the log-spaced run with 164 time steps; the reduced model as the best of 20 runs; the full system with its matrices solved directly (one factorisation, 1 s steps). Against that direct solution the reduced model is still about 9000 times faster. Above 100 s the CalculiX curve is the run with 2.5 s time steps, and the reduced model in your browser uses the same steps.
